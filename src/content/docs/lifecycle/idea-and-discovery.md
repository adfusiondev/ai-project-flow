The project lifecycle begins with an idea and the discovery work needed to understand it.

## What this stage is

Discovery is the process of clarifying what you are building, why it exists, who it is for, and what outcome it should produce. It turns a vague or incomplete idea into a clear enough picture to plan the rest of the project.

## Why it matters

Assumptions made early in a project are expensive to correct later. A short discovery stage prevents the rest of the lifecycle from being built on a misunderstood idea.

## What to clarify

- What is being built?
- Why does it exist and what problem does it solve?
- Who will use it — is it personal, internal, public, or commercial?
- What product form should it take — website, application, CLI, API, or other?
- What platforms or environments must it support?
- What is the main user flow or workflow?
- What is clearly out of scope?

## What should be completed before moving on

- The project purpose is clear enough to document.
- The primary user is identified.
- The product form is decided.
- Important unknowns are documented rather than assumed.

## When it is complete

You can move to **Project Context** when the idea can be stated in a few clear sentences and the remaining questions are about scope rather than purpose.
