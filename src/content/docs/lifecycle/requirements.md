Requirements translate the approved MVP into a precise specification that can be built and verified.

## What this stage is

This stage writes down what the project must do (functional requirements), how it must behave (non-functional requirements), and any constraints the MVP must respect.

## Why it matters

Requirements are the reference point for both implementation and verification. They prevent the MVP from growing silently and make it possible to check whether the finished work is correct.

## What to document

- **Functional requirements** — what the website or product must do.
- **Non-functional requirements** — simplicity, readability, responsiveness, performance, accessibility, portability.
- **Content requirements** — how content should be written and structured.
- **Design requirements** — layout and visual expectations.
- **Localization requirements** — supported languages and text direction.
- **Portability requirements** — how the project must remain understandable across environments.
- **MVP constraints** — what the MVP must not require.

## What should be completed before moving on

- `docs/REQUIREMENTS.md` exists.
- Requirements match the MVP.
- Important behavior is clear.
- Constraints are documented.
- Requirements can later be verified.

Move to **Architecture** when the requirements are specific enough to design against.
